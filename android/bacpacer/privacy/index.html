bacpacer privacy policy

Applies to the bacpacer app for Android phones and its Wear OS watch app. Last updated: 18 November 2026.

In short: bacpacer does not collect, share or sell any data. Everything you enter stays on your own devices: your phone, and your watch if you use the bacpacer watch app.

What is stored

The drinks you log (name, amount, alcohol content and time) and your settings (body weight, height, year of birth, body type, metabolism, food, pace, units, clock and blood alcohol limit). They are stored only in the app's private storage on your phone.

If you use the bacpacer watch app, the watch keeps a copy of what it needs: your drinks from about the last day, the name, amount and alcohol content of your three most recent different drinks (without times), your body profile, food, pace, limit, unit and clock settings, and your drink presets, plus drinks you log on the watch until they reach the phone.

What leaves your phone

Without the bacpacer watch app: nothing.

With the watch app installed, your phone and watch share the data listed above through Google Play services (the Wearable Data Layer). Google Play services may pass it through Google's servers, for example when the watch is connected over Wi-Fi instead of Bluetooth; Google's privacy policy ↗ (opens in a new tab) applies to that.

bacpacer itself does not request the internet permission and never sends data to the developer or anyone else. There are no accounts, ads, analytics, crash reporters or trackers. Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled for this app.

Deleting your data

Delete single drinks from the list, or everything at once with Settings → Delete all my data. Deleting all data on the phone also clears the drinks and profile the watch shows. Uninstalling the app also removes all of its data; uninstalling the watch app removes the watch's copy. Because the developer never receives your data, there is nothing to delete on our side.

Children

bacpacer is meant for adults of legal drinking age.

Not a medical device

bacpacer shows a rough estimate of blood alcohol content based on a general formula. It does not measure anything and must never be used to decide whether you can drive or do anything else that needs you to be fully alert.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
